# Loss Aversion

**Source**: Kahneman & Tversky, Prospect Theory (1979).

**Awareness stage fit**: problem aware, solution aware. Fails on unaware readers: they must already recognize the loss.

## Mechanism

People feel losses roughly 2x more intensely than equivalent gains. Frame the message around what the reader is losing by not having the product, not what they gain by having it.

## When it works

- The product solves a measurable waste (time, money, effort).
- The audience already experiences the pain but has not quantified it.
- You have a real number to anchor the loss.

## When it fails

- The loss is vague or exaggerated. "You're losing everything!" feels manipulative.
- The audience does not recognize the problem yet. They need awareness first, not loss framing.

## Pattern

`[Audience] waste/lose [specific amount] on [problem] that [product] solves in [smaller amount]`

## Examples

- "You're wasting 30 minutes a week on Chrome tasks you could do in 5."
- "Every month without this, your team loses 12 hours to manual data entry."
- "The average developer mass-closes 40 browser tabs a day. That is 3 hours of lost context per week."
