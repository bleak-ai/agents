# Contrast Against Alternative

**Source**: positioning practice (Ries and Trout, Positioning, 1981; April
Dunford, Obviously Awesome, 2019). Define the product by what the known
alternatives cannot do.

**Awareness stage fit**: solution aware, product aware. The reader must
already know the alternative being contrasted.

## Mechanism

Name a category the audience already uses, acknowledge what it does well,
then show the gap the product fills. The known alternative does the
explaining; the headline only has to mark the difference.

## Input

Read the "Positioning vs alternatives" section of `products/<slug>/info.md`.
Only contrast against alternatives listed there. Never invent a weakness.

## When it works

- The audience uses or knows the alternative.
- The acknowledged strength is genuine. Fair contrast reads as competence.

## When it fails

- Trashing the alternative. Technical audiences defend tools they use.
- The alternative is obscure and the contrast explains nothing.

## Pattern

`[Alternative] gives you [its strength]. It cannot [the gap]. [Product]
[fills the gap].`
`[Alternative] is [fair label]. [Product] is [contrasting label].`

## Examples

- "CLAUDE.md is where your agent context starts. It is not where it should
  stay."
- "Auto memory remembers for you. A context folder remembers with you."
- "RAG retrieves chunks. gcontext keeps knowledge you can open in an
  editor."
