# Curiosity Gap

**Source**: George Loewenstein, "The Psychology of Curiosity" (1994). Supported by Kahneman's System 1 research: curiosity activates the same reward circuits as hunger.

**Awareness stage fit**: unaware, problem aware.

## Mechanism

Create a gap between what the reader knows and what they want to know. The headline opens the gap; the post closes it.

## When it works

- The reveal is genuinely surprising or useful. The reader must feel rewarded for clicking.
- The gap is specific enough that the reader can imagine the answer space ("what is the trick?") but cannot guess it.

## When it fails

- The gap is too vague ("you won't believe this").
- The payoff does not match the promise. Clickbait is curiosity gap without delivery.
- Technical audiences resist it if it feels manipulative.

## Pattern

`[Surprising claim or question] that [implies valuable knowledge]`

## Examples

- "The one Chrome setting that 90% of developers never change (and why it costs them 20 min/day)."
- "I replaced 5 browser extensions with 3 lines of JavaScript."
- "Why the fastest way to close 100 tabs is not what you think."

## Honesty rule

The post must deliver on the curiosity gap. If the answer inside is obvious or disappointing, the reader feels tricked. Write the payoff first, then write the headline. If the payoff is weak, pick a different formula.
