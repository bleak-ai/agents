# Authority

**Source**: Cialdini, Influence (1984). People trust credible sources.

**Awareness stage fit**: any stage. Strongest when the claim is unfamiliar and needs credibility to be heard.

## Mechanism

Show credentials, experience, or domain expertise. The same content becomes more persuasive when the reader knows the author has standing.

## When it works

- The author has real experience in the domain (years, role, results).
- The authority is relevant to the product. A surgeon's endorsement matters for medical tools, not for a task manager.
- The credential is specific: "10 years building browser extensions" beats "experienced developer".

## When it fails

- The authority is self-proclaimed with no evidence.
- The credential does not match the domain.
- It sounds like bragging instead of context.

## Pattern

`[Credential/experience] + [what I built/found/learned]`

## Examples

- "After 10 years of building Chrome extensions, I compiled everything into one cookbook."
- "I manage 200+ browser tabs daily for my research. Here is the system I built."
- "We ran 500 A/B tests on landing pages last year. Here are the 7 patterns that actually moved the needle."
