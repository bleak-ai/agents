# Number + Anchor

**Source**: Kahneman, Thinking Fast and Slow (2011). System 2 needs concrete data to evaluate a claim. Numbers stop System 1 because they are unexpected in a sea of words.

**Awareness stage fit**: problem aware, solution aware, product aware.

## Mechanism

Lead with a specific number. The number anchors the reader's expectations and makes the claim feel concrete and credible.

## When it works

- The number is real, specific, and impressive (or surprisingly small).
- The number is in the headline, not buried in the body.
- Combined with another lever (number + loss, number + identity).

## When it fails

- The number is rounded or vague ("save hours" instead of "save 2.3 hours").
- The number is unverifiable and the audience is skeptical.

## Pattern

`[Specific number] [concrete outcome] [in timeframe/context]`

## Examples

- "50 Chrome tricks that save me 3 hours every week."
- "I went from 200 open tabs to 12. Here is the system."
- "7 lines of code that replaced my $50/month subscription."

## Combined formula (strongest variant)

`[Specific number] + [loss frame] + [identity signal]`

Example: "Chrome power users waste 2+ hours/week on stuff this free cookbook solves in clicks."
- Specific number (System 2)
- Loss frame (loss aversion)
- Identity signal ("Chrome power users" = unity)
- "Free" (reciprocity trigger)
