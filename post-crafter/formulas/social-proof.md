# Social Proof

**Source**: Cialdini, Influence (1984). Replicated in Cialdini's hotel towel study: specific social proof outperforms generic appeals by 40%.

**Awareness stage fit**: solution aware, product aware.

## Mechanism

People follow what others do, especially others like them. Show numbers, adoption, or endorsement from peers.

## When it works

- You have real metrics (users, downloads, stars, companies).
- The numbers are specific, not rounded. "1,247 developers" beats "thousands of developers".
- The social proof comes from the reader's peer group, not a generic crowd.

## When it fails

- The numbers are small and you try to spin them. "Join our 12 users" is honest but weak.
- The proof is from a group the audience does not identify with.

## Pattern

`[Specific number] [peer group] already [action] with [product]`

## Examples

- "500 Chrome power users downloaded this in the first week."
- "Used by developers at 3 of the top 10 YC companies."
- "1,247 teams switched from [alternative] in the last quarter."
