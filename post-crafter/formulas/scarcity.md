# Scarcity

**Source**: Cialdini, Influence (1984). Works best when something available becomes scarce, not when it was always scarce.

**Awareness stage fit**: product aware only. Urgency means nothing to a reader who does not want the product yet.

## Mechanism

People value what is rare or disappearing. Limited time, limited access, or limited quantity creates urgency.

## When it works

- The scarcity is real (early access, limited beta spots, time-bound offer).
- The transition from available to scarce is visible ("was open, now closing").
- Combined with social proof ("87 of 100 spots taken").

## When it fails

- The scarcity is fake. Readers detect manufactured urgency fast, especially technical audiences.
- Overused. If everything is "limited", nothing is.

## Pattern

`[Limited thing] [time/quantity constraint] [what you get]`

## Examples

- "Early access closes Friday. 200 spots, 143 taken."
- "Free during beta. Pricing starts next month."
- "First 50 signups get lifetime access at the beta price."

## Honesty rule

Only use this formula when the scarcity is true. Do not create fake deadlines or invent limited spots. Technical audiences will call it out and the product loses trust permanently.
